## Abstract
Preventing bacterial biofilms is crucial for maintaining water-associated infrastructure and minimizing biofouling. Biofilm formation is a significant contributor to biofouling, resulting in surface deterioration and leading to reduced operational efficiency in water-related systems. Zwitterionic amphiphilic copolymers (ZACs) combine a zwitterionic monomer unit with a hydrophobic monomer component and are well known for resisting organic fouling. However, their effects on bacterial biofouling are more complex. Understanding these interactions is essential for the rational design of next-generation antifouling materials. In this study, we investigated the antibiofilm performance of ZAC coatings against Vibrio fischeri , a well-established marine model organism for biofilm research. ZACs were prepared by copolymerizing the zwitterionic monomer 2-methacryloyloxyethyl phosphorylcholine (MPC) with either methyl methacrylate (PM: MPC) or trifluoroethyl methacrylate (PT: MPC) and compared with their corresponding homopolymer controls. The coatings containing varying MPC contents (10-30wt%) were initially screened, revealing that 30wt% MPC provided the most effective biofilm resistance and was therefore selected for detailed structural and molecular analyses. Confocal microscopy demonstrated that PM: MPC and PT: MPC coatings with 30wt% MPC reduced biofilm biomass and thickness by 90% relative to uncoated controls at 72 h, with PT: MPC 70:30 achieving the strongest overall performance (94% inhibition by crystal violet assay; 92% reduction in confocal biomass volume). Gene expression profiling further revealed strong downregulation of biofilm-associated genes on ZAC surfaces, with PT: MPC showing the most sustained suppression over the 72-hour period. Together, these results demonstrate that MPC-containing ZAC coatings strongly inhibit biofilm development and show strong potential as antifouling surfaces for water-related applications.
