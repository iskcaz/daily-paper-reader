## Abstract
The permanganate/peroxymonosulfate (PM/PMS) oxidation process has recently attracted considerable attention as a promising and sustainable technology for the treatment of saline shale gas wastewater containing emerging pollutants. However, the effects of aqueous matrix components, particularly chloride ions (Cl⁻), on its oxidation mechanisms and performance remain insufficiently understood. In this study, sulfamethoxazole (SMX) was selected as a representative antibiotic to investigate the enhancement mechanisms and environmental implications of Cl⁻ in the PM/PMS system. Results revealed that Cl⁻ significantly enhanced SMX degradation in a concentration-dependent manner by facilitating PMS activation to produce hypochlorous acid (HClO), accelerating the conversion of PM into Mn(VI) and MnO₂, and mediating the transformation of reactive oxygen species (ROS, e.g., SO4•⁻ and HO•) into reactive chlorine species (RCS, e.g., Cl• and Cl₂•⁻). Additionally, Cl⁻ enhanced SMX degradation by promoting HClO/RCS formation, mediating ROS-to-RCS transformation, and coupling these processes with reactive manganese species cycling, thereby establishing a ROS-RMnS-RCS ternary oxidation network. Liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) and density functional theory (DFT) analyses indicated that SMX degradation mainly occurred through amino oxidation, S-N/S-C bond cleavage, and chlorination reactions. Toxicity assessments using ECOSAR modeling and wheat germination assays demonstrated that the degradation products exhibited lower ecotoxicity than the parent SMX. Experiments with actual chlorinated shale gas wastewater further verified the robustness and adaptability of the PM/PMS oxidation process. This work provides mechanistic insight into the Cl⁻-mediated regulation of PM/PMS oxidation and highlights its potential as a clean and efficient strategy for antibiotic pollution control in saline shale gas wastewater treatment, contributing to sustainable water resource management in energy-related industries.
