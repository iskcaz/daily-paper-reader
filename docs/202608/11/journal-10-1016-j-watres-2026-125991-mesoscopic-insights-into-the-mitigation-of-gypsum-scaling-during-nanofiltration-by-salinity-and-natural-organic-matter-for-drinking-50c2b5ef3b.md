## Abstract
Nanofiltration (NF) plays a pivotal role in producing high-quality drinking water, but gypsum scaling is a key obstacle for NF applications. This study systematically evaluated the combined effects of salinity and natural organic matter (NOM) on gypsum scaling in NF systems. Results show that elevated salinity (0-10 g/L) enhanced CaSO4 solubility and suppressed homogeneous nucleation, increasing water recovery from 55.5% to 70.0% and final normalized flux from 0.12 to 0.40. In the presence of NOM, 10 mg/L humic acid (HA)/sodium alginate (SA) inhibited ordered crystal growth via complexation, coating and steric hindrance, yielding recoveries of 61.0/66.5% and fluxes of 0.15/0.18. Higher HA/SA concentrations (50 mg/L) formed a dynamic protective layer, transforming needle-like crystals into uniform organic-inorganic deposits with normalized fluxes of 0.33/0.25 at 70.0% recovery. Normalized flux >0.30 at 70.0% recovery was achieved in presence of both salinity and NOM. Thermodynamic and kinetic analyses show that combined salinity and HA/SA regulated bulk nucleation (ΔG*, 7.10-58.05 kJ/mol), zeta potential (-19.72 to -8.24 mV), surface tension (35.50-69.10 mJ/m²) and interaction energy ( [Formula: see text] , -44.44 to -1.76 mJ/m²), forming a multi-barrier mechanism that prevents NF scaling. These molecular dynamics (MD)-validated insights inform the optimization of NF operation via salinity-NOM synergy to enhance scaling resistance and prolong membrane lifespan.
