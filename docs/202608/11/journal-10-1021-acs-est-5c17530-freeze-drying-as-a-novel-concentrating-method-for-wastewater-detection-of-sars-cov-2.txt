Abstract 8

Detecting viral RNA from wastewater has emerged as a cost-effective approach for community-9

level surveillance during the recent SARS-CoV-2 pandemic. Although various concentrating 10 

methods have been developed, none are optimal for all key requirements for wastewater viral 11 

detection. Freeze-drying, a technique widely used for concentrating and preserving biological 12 

materials, remains underexplored for this purpose. This study compared the performance of 13 

freeze-drying and centrifugal ultrafiltration in terms of recovery efficiency, detection limit, and 14 

other key parameters. Early pandemic samples in this study, with extremely low viral 15 

concentrations, offered an ideal benchmark to assess their suitability for early-warning 16 

applications. Statistical analyses showed that freeze-drying achieved significantly higher 17 

recovery efficiency (20.2 ± 3.8% vs. 12.9 ± 2.0%), superior detection ratio (68.4% vs. 31.6%), 18 

and lower detection limit (0.06 vs. 0.35 copies/mL) compared to centrifugal ultrafiltration. To 19 

our knowledge, this is the first study to apply freeze-drying for wastewater-based viral detection. 20 

Despite its longer processing time, freeze-drying offers multiple advantages, including 21 

elimination of pretreatment steps, flexible storage, reduced RNA degradation under cryogenic 22 

conditions, minimal pathogen exposure, lower labor demands, and less human interference 23 

during processing. These features position freeze-drying as a novel alternative for wastewater-24

1. Introduction 32 

Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) is caused by the Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome 33 

Coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2), a single-stranded RNA virus. The World Health Organization has 34 

classified COVID-19 as a global pandemic. As of December 15, 2024, over 7 million deaths 35 

have been reported worldwide. 1,2 Timely detection and monitoring of the virus at the early stages 36 

of a pandemic are crucial to mitigate its spread, and wastewater surveillance has emerged as a 37 

recognized and effective approach. Notably, in March 2020, Medema et al. reported the first 38 

detection of SARS-CoV-2 RNA in wastewater in the Netherlands, preceding major outbreaks in 39 

various regions. 3-5 Quantifying SARS-CoV-2 viral RNA in sewage offers the possibility for 40 

developing an early alarm system, providing valuable insights into disease prevalence, and 41 

allowing public health authorities more time to intervene. Thus, a sensitive and cost-effective 42 

method will be desirable for such applications. 43 

Nevertheless, assessing viral load within wastewater presents challenges due to the variable 44 

composition of sewage. Wastewater comprises both liquid and solid phases, each might contain 45 

viral signals. 3 Moreover, the composition of wastewater is influenced by numerous factors, 46

nucleases in the environment can potentially degrade nucleic acids, particularly RNA, 49 

representing another significant factor impacting detectable viral RNA concentration in 50 

wastewater. 5 Considering all these variables, detecting minute quantities of specific pathogens in 51 

the complex matrix of wastewater represents a substantial challenge, necessitating a proper 52 

sample concentrating method to maximize preservation of signal for subsequent detection. 53 

To tackle these challenges, a concentrating method should be developed specifically to address 54 

the properties of wastewater. An effective method must efficiently collect viruses from both 55 

wastewater phases while protecting viral genomic materials from degradation. The method 56 

should avoid (1) pretreatments such as prefiltration that could result in loss of the viruses 57 

associated with solid particles; 6,7 (2) addition of chemicals that may inhibit downstream 58 

detection. 8–10 Additionally, especially for RNA viruses, the approach should minimize the 59 

degradation caused by ubiquitous environmental RNases. 11 Given the very low viral load during 60 

early and late pandemic stages ( Figure 1 a), the recovery rate and detection limit of concentrating 61 

method will be the most critical criterion. For resource-limited facilities such as those wastewater 62 

treatment plants, the concentrating method should be easy to establish without specialized 63 

personnel training. In addition, labor-intensive processes should be minimized in the protocol for 64 

long-term surveillance applications. 12 65 

The U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) has outlined five distinct 66 

methodologies for concentrating viral particles from wastewater. 13 While each of these methods 67 

employs different principles to address the challenges of low viral RNA content in 68 

wastewater, 6,14 –19 none fully meet all the criteria for effectively concentrating and detecting 69

onset of COVID-19 peaks with sporadic infections, necessitates the development of more 71 

strategic sample concentrating methods. 72 

In contrast, freeze-drying, which is not listed by CDC, is a promising alternative concentrating 73 

method that is widely used to preserve various biological products. 20,21 Recently, it has been 74 

applied to concentrate wastewater samples to evaluate the deactivating effect of Fe 3+ treatment 75 

on bacteria, 22 yet its potential for wastewater-based virus detection has not been explored. 76 

Freeze-drying effectively removes water from wastewater matrices by sublimation. This method 77 

uniquely retains viral entities from both the liquid and solid components of wastewater, which 78 

enhances detection sensitivity. Additionally, freeze-drying does not rely on reagent-dependent 79 

sample pre-treatment, thus preserving sample integrity from chemical interventions. The 80 

cryogenic environment during freeze-drying prevents the degradation of viral RNA, thereby 81 

improving the detection limit in the subsequent steps. Although it is time-consuming to process 82 

large-volume samples, freeze-drying offers operational convenience by minimizing human 83 

oversight after initial setup. In summary, freeze-drying represents a novel, sensitive, reliable, and 84 

less labor-intensive virus detection methodology, as it minimizes viral loss and optimizes the 85 

protection of viral RNA. Nevertheless, its effectiveness needs to be assessed by comparing to 86 

existing methods. 87

This study will be the first to apply freeze-drying to measure SARS-CoV-2 levels in 88 

wastewater. A comparative analysis was conducted between freeze-drying and widely used 89 

centrifugal ultrafiltration as concentrating methods for wastewater samples. Throughout the 90 

subsequent sections, “ultrafiltration” will be used in lieu of the more descriptive term 91 

"centrifugal ultrafiltration" to improve conciseness. Ultrafiltration was chosen for comparison 92 

because it is among the most widely used methods, having been employed in the first 23 and 93 

several early publications 24 –26 on detecting SARS-CoV-2 in wastewater. Critical factors, 94 

including recovery efficiency and detection limits, were systematically compared and evaluated 95 

through RT-qPCR analyses across both concentrating methods. 96 

The wastewater samples tested in this study held exceptional value as they were collected 97 

during the critical onset and waning phases of pandemic peaks. During these periods, viral 98 

concentrations were extremely low, highlighting the value of this comparison in selecting a 99 

better concentrating method for developing a wastewater-based early warning system. Before 100 

using these precious samples, thorough method testing and optimization have been carried out 101 

using DNA and RNA standards, as well as a similar but less pathogenic coronavirus. Although 102 

each invaluable sample was only processed by each concentrating method once due to volume 103 

limitation of sample collected in those pandemic periods, our optimal utilization strategy ensured 104 

reliable quantitative measurements while maximizing the extraction of meaningful 105 

epidemiological insights from these irreplaceable specimens. The resulting data are pivotal for 106 

developing a sensitive, precise, reproducible, and practical system to detect viruses in 107 

wastewater, ultimately enhancing community-level early warning and surveillance capabilities. 108

2. Material and Methods 109 

2.1. Sample Collection and Pasteurization 110 

Raw wastewater samples composited over 24 hours from the final effluent were collected on 111 

designated dates primarily during the early stages of the first COVID-19 pandemic peak in El Paso, 112 

Texas, USA, with additional samples collected during the low and steady tail of the peak ( Figure 113 

1a, Supplementary Table S3 ). Due to limited storage space in -80 °C freezers, only 500 mL of 114 

each sample was obtained. The wastewater samples were collected from four wastewater treatment 115 

plants (WWTPs) in El Paso, Texas, USA. These WWTPs are Haskell Street Wastewater Plant 116 

(HS), John T. Hickerson Water Reclamation Facility (JH), Fred Hervey Water Reclamation (FH), 117 

and Roberto R. Bustamante Wastewater Plant (BU). Locations of these WWTPs are shown in 118 

Figure 1 c. Due to safety concerns during the early phase of the pandemic, before the person-to-119 

person transmission pathway of the SARS-CoV-2 was elucidated (March 2020 to July 2020), a 120 

sample collecting protocol was developed and approved by the Institutional Biosafety Committee 121 

(IBC) of the University of Texas at El Paso (UTEP). Individuals collecting samples overcautiously 122 

wore double-layer disposable nitrile gloves, splash goggles, N95 or surgical masks, and disposable 123 

lab coats. Nevertheless, these overcautiously designed sample collection protocols can be used for 124 

future emerging viruses. 125

2.6. COVID-19 Case Data and Statistical Analysis 301 

Daily COVID-19 records from April 14, 2020, to May 28, 2021, in El Paso, TX, was collected 302 

from  https://www.epstrong.org/results.php  and https://elpasocovid19tracker.com/index.html 303 

(accessed 2025-11-18). Sampling dates with associated viral load calculations from all four 304 

wastewater plant sites are listed in Supplementary Table S3 . The correlation between the total 305 

COVID-19 cases and SARS-CoV-2 viral load in wastewater was first evaluated for normality 306 

using the Shapiro-Wilk test. 33 Based on the results of the test, the Spearman’s rank correlation 307 

was calculated between the total COVID-19 cases and SARS-CoV-2 viral load in wastewater, 308 

which can be denoted as rho “ ρ” .34 This ρ, ranging from -1 to 1, provided a quantitative measure 309 

of the observed relationship between the two variables: daily COVID-19 case numbers and 310 

SARS-CoV-2 viral load in wastewater. A ρ close to zero means no correlation, while close to 1 311 

and -1 indicate strong positive or negative correlation, respectively. All data processing and 312 

statistical analyses were conducted using R Studio. 35 313 

3. Results 314 

3.1. Sample Collection 315 

Our study underscores the importance of strict safety procedures when handling wastewater 316 

samples containing emerging pathogens, particularly in adhering to the stringent biosafety 317

Standard curve analysis in  Figure 3  shows that across all points of RNA and DNA standards, 358

sample storage, for which TRIzol or similar denaturing reagents are highly recommended. 379 

TRIzol effectively inhibits RNase activity, thereby preventing RNA degradation. 37 Concentrated 380 

samples in TRIzol demonstrate superior stability for long-term storage, particularly when stored 381 

at -80 °C freezer. 38 Thus, maintaining samples in TRIzol without RNA extraction until imminent 382 

RT-qPCR analysis is considered the optimal storage strategy. Moreover, minimizing freeze-thaw 383 

cycles of samples, regardless of the storage method, is critical to prevent RNA degradation. 384 

RNases are prevalent and can regain enzymatic activity after removing denaturing conditions. 39 385 

Consequently, after RNA is extracted from the TRIzol storage solution, downstream procedures 386 

should be performed promptly to minimize RNA degradation and ensure the reliability and 387 

accuracy of the subsequent RT-qPCR analyses. 388

3.4. Comparison of Early Detection of Two Concentrating Methods 419 

To evaluate the detection sensitivities of the freeze-drying and ultrafiltration methods, 420 

wastewater viral load data from the early stages of the COVID-19 pandemic (April 2020 to May 421 

2020) were analyzed. A total of 38 samples were collected from various wastewater treatment 422 

plants in El Paso, Texas, USA. The freeze-drying method identified 26 positive samples (68.4%) 423 

(Table 2 ). As previously discussed, the freeze-drying method yielded lower Ct values (indicating 424 

more sensitive detection) in the second elutions. When the second elution was included, the 425 

detection ratio increased to 28 positive samples (73.7%). In contrast, the ultrafiltration detected 426 

only 12 positive samples (31.6%), which increased slightly to 13 positive samples (34.2%) when 427 

including the second elution ( Table 2 ). The higher detection ratio observed with the freeze-428 

drying method highlights its superior ability to concentrate viral material compared to 429 

ultrafiltration. This is particularly important given that our samples were collected during the 430 

early stages of the pandemic, when viral loads in wastewater were extremely low ( Figure 1 a). 431 

The higher detection ratio observed with the freeze-drying method suggests it is a more effective 432 

approach for establishing an early warning system for the surveillance of emerging viruses. 433 

The superior detection sensitivity of the freeze-drying method was further supported by the 434 

lower average Ct values obtained during RT-qPCR analyses. Across all four wastewater 435 

treatment plants, the freeze-drying method consistently exhibited lower Ct values compared to 436 

ultrafiltration ( Table 2 ), equivalent to a higher amount of viral RNA in the concentrated samples 437 

(Supplementary Table S3 ). Furthermore, a Wilcoxon rank-sum test, a nonparametric test that 438 

compares two independent groups without assumption of normal distribution, 40 was conducted to 439 

compare virus copies in the original wastewater samples across all four facilities. As described in 440 

Section  2.4 , the total virus copies were estimated using the DNA standard curve and the Ct 441

3.5. Correlation Between Wastewater Viral Load and COVID-19 Case Numbers 456 

The relationship between wastewater viral load and community COVID-19 cases is a key 457 

consideration in evaluating the utility of wastewater surveillance. The correlation between the 458 

measured viral load in wastewater and the reported COVID-19 cases in El Paso, TX, USA, was 459 

investigated. Available viral load data from Haskell Street Wastewater Plant and the 460 

corresponding daily COVID-19 case counts on the wastewater sample collection dates were 461 

collected. The areas from where the case numbers were collected cover HS serving regions, 462 

including zip codes 79901, 79902, 79903, 79905, and 79930 ( Figure 1 c). 463 

Normalization of viral load data to accurately account for differences in human contribution to 464 

wastewater is widely considered essential in wastewater surveillance. Some studies use 465 

creatinine as a normalization biomarker to improve the correlation between wastewater viral 466 

loads and COVID-19 case trends. 41,42 Attempts following these studies were carried out via 467 

creatinine measurement using a commercial colorimetric kit ( Supplementary Section 1.5 ). 468 

However, the concentrations of creatinine measured in the collected samples ( Supplementary 469 

Section 1.5 ) were consistently below the kit's sensitivity limit, preventing reliable normalization, 470 

similar to the other report. 41 Given these analytical constraints and the primary goal of 471 

comparing two concentrating methods, the correlation analysis in this study was conducted on 472 

unnormalized data. Therefore, the correlation results presented here are meant to provide 473 

supplemental insight into the relationship between wastewater viral concentrations and 474 

community COVID-19 case counts, rather than to serve as definitive epidemiological findings. 475 

Shapiro-Wilk tests showed p = 5.6 × 10 -6 , 8.4 × 10 -5 , and 1.2 × 10 -5 , for total RNA copies 476 

detected by freeze-drying, those detected by ultrafiltration, and community COVID-19 case 477 

numbers, respectively. Thus, it was concluded that these data were not normally distributed. 478

Given the small sample size, a non-parametric test was chosen instead of a parametric test. 33,43 479 

The Spearman rank correlation test 34 was applied to evaluate the association between the total 480 

viral load and the number of reported daily cases on each date across the studied areas. Due to 481 

the unavailability of active case numbers during the early stages of the pandemic (April 2020 to 482

studies have indicated that recovered patients may continue shedding the virus. 44,45 During the 485 

early period, the analysis revealed a positive correlation of 0.45 between the freeze-dried viral 486 

load and the daily case numbers. This correlation was statistically significant (p=0.048). 487 

Similarly, for the ultrafiltration method, using data from the same period, the analysis showed a 488 

moderate positive correlation of 0.24 between the viral load and the cumulative COVID-19 case 489 

numbers, which was not statistically significant (p=0.305). Our results are consistent with 490 

various correlations found in published studies, some demonstrated a positive correlation 23,46 491 

while other studies have reported weaker or inconsistent correlations 47 . These variations are 492 

potentially due to factors such as differences in viral shedding patterns and in testing rates, as 493 

well as the influence of local conditions on viral transport and persistence in wastewater 494 

systems. 48 It is important to note that the correlation observed in this study is mostly based on 495 

minimal data from the early stages of the pandemic and lacks reliable normalization of human 496 

contribution to wastewater. 497

4. Discussion 499 

The two concentrating methods, ultrafiltration and freeze-drying, exhibited distinct 500 

disadvantages in performance and methodology in this study. Ultrafiltration requires multiple 501 

> All rights reserved. No reuse allowed without permission. (which was not certified by peer review) is the author/funder, who has granted medRxiv a license to display the preprint in perpetuity. The copyright holder for this preprint this version posted December 5, 2025. ;https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.01.04.25319877 doi: medRxiv preprint

pretreatment steps, such as centrifugation and filtration, to remove larger solids. However, this 502 

process can inadvertently lead to the loss of viral signals that are associated with sewage 503 

sludge. 49,50 This was confirmed by the viral signal detected in the pellet from the prefiltration 504 

step ( Supplementary Table S2 ). The same observation was also reported in literature, where 505 

viral detection rate is commonly decreased when solids are excluded from wastewater samples. 51 506 

On the other hand, the major disadvantage of freeze-drying is its long processing time, where 507 

one overnight deep-freezing process and around three days are needed to concentrate a 200 mL 508 

sample to a target volume of approximately 3 mL. 509 

Although freeze-drying has a longer processing time, it demonstrated a superior recovery rate 510 

and lower detection limit compared to ultrafiltration in this study. This makes freeze-drying 511 

particularly advantageous for early detection of viral pathogens during the onset or waning 512 

phases of a pandemic ( Figure 1 a) when viral loads in wastewater are extremely low. This 513 

superior performance can be attributed to several factors. Unlike ultrafiltration, freeze-drying 514 

eliminates the need for prefiltration by retaining all wastewater components, including solids, 515 

thereby maximizing viral capture, particularly for viruses associated with sewage sludge that 516 

would otherwise be lost in the pretreatment of centrifugation and filtration. The process of 517 

freeze-drying involves fewer liquid handling steps, reducing the opportunity for sample loss and 518 

contamination. In addition, the immediate freezing of samples at −80 °C after pasteurization and 519 

maintaining the sample throughout concentrating at ultra-low temperatures reduces the 520 

degradation of viral RNA and preserves its integrity prior to downstream processing. This is 521 

most important for RNA viruses due to the ubiquitous existence of RNase in the environment. 522 

Additionally, the improved retention and processing fidelity achieved by freeze-drying is 523 

> All rights reserved. No reuse allowed without permission. (which was not certified by peer review) is the author/funder, who has granted medRxiv a license to display the preprint in perpetuity. The copyright holder for this preprint this version posted December 5, 2025. ;https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.01.04.25319877 doi: medRxiv preprint

reflected in stronger correlations between wastewater viral loads and community case data, 524 

supporting its reliability for tracking disease prevalence through wastewater surveillance. 525 

From the operational aspects, ultrafiltration uses expensive filters and involves multiple 526 

centrifugation and liquid handling steps. These steps prolong operator exposure to wastewater 527 

and increase pathogen transmission risks. Consequently, when working with unknown emerging 528 

pathogens, ultrafiltration requires rigorous personal protective equipment (PPE) and extensive 529 

decontamination procedures, resulting in labor-intensive workflows and limiting the throughput 530 

to approximately eight samples per day. In contrast, the freeze-drying method uses only 531 

inexpensive sample bottles and minimizes operator exposure, requiring brief handling for cap 532 

loosening and flask mounting ( Figure 2 ) during the initial setup. Decontamination was only 533 

needed at process completion. Although the freeze-drying method does require three days to 534 

concentrate eight samples, it demands minimal labor, reduces exposure to wastewater, and 535 

minimizes human-factor variability during the whole process period ( Section  2.3 ). 536 

In summary, using invaluable samples collected mainly during the onset of the COVID-19 537 

pandemic, this study conclusively demonstrated the superior performance of freeze-drying 538 

method over the widely used ultrafiltration technique for concentrating wastewater samples for 539 

viral detection. Freeze-drying ’s higher recovery rates, detection ratios, and sensitivity for viral 540 

RNA in wastewater are crucial during periods when viral loads are low, such as the early phase 541 

of a pandemic. Freeze-drying’s operational benefits include increased storage flexibility, reduced 542 

labor intensity, minimized exposure risks are particularly suitable for settings with limited 543 

technical expertise. All these advantages of freeze-drying position it as a novel sensitive, precise, 544 

reproducible, and practical method for early warning and surveillance applications against 545 

emerging pathogens in the community. 546
