## Abstract
While peroxymonosulfate-based advanced oxidation processes (AOPs) are effective for pollutant removal, designing catalysts that combine high activity, stability, and efficient electron transfer remains challenging. Herein, we synthesized a series of carbon-stabilized high-entropy alloys (HEAs) catalysts (MnFeCoNiCu/xZn@C) for activating peroxymonosulfate (PMS) to degrade tetracycline (TC). The volatile nature of Zn was utilized during pyrolysis to tailor lattice distortion and optimize interparticle voids within the face-centered cubic (FCC) HEAs structure on a carbon framework. The optimized MnFeCoNiCu/4Zn@C displayed smaller lattice spacing, higher lattice parameters and lower average pore size to majorize lattice distortion. The catalyst exhibits excellent PMS activation for tetracycline degradation with exceeding 95% efficiency within 30 min, a mineralization rate of 30% at 300 mg/L TC, universal degradation properties toward diverse antibiotics and strong resistance against prevalent inorganic ions. Even after ten cycles, the removal efficiency stabilizes an efficiency of over 85%, attributes to the stability of the HEAs. Quenching experiments and electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) identified sulfate radical (SO4•-) as the dominant reactive species. Density functional theory (DFT) calculations revealed that the multi-alloys synergy in the HEAs significantly enhances electron transfer, facilitating PMS adsorption and activation. Based on identification of intermediates, three plausible degradation pathways were proposed. Toxicity assessment indicated a general reduction in ecotoxicity as degradation progressed. Life cycle assessment (LCA) proved the overall sustainability of HEAs/PMS system. This study synergistically combines the utilization of lattice distortion and high-entropy characteristics to achieve the design of a novel catalyst for PMS-based AOPs (PMS-AOPs).
